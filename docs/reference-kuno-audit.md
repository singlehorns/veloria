# K.UNO Reference Audit

Reference: https://www.k-uno.com.tw/

## Observed Structure

- Header uses a two-level composition: centered brand mark on the first row, reservation CTA on the right, and a separated navigation row below.
- The first viewport is image-led. A large visual sits left and center, while the right side keeps a pale editorial panel with a vertical scroll cue.
- Hero copy is vertical and compact. The message is not a large horizontal headline; it behaves like a refined editorial label layered over the image.
- Page sections are organized around repeated modules: brand introduction, craftsmanship/ordermade stories, collection links, pick up/news, column, best sellers, service, and reservation.
- Section titles typically use an English label or simple heading, with content kept in restrained sans-serif body text.
- Interactions are subtle: soft reveal, restrained hover states, carousel arrows, and clear CTA affordances.

## Transfer Principles For Diamond Symphony

- Keep Diamond Symphony products, images, links, and brand language.
- Use the K.UNO rhythm as a layout reference, not a content source.
- Prioritize a stable top-to-bottom reading path: Hero, brand introduction, recommendation, knowledge, featured collections, product categories, consultation.
- Use the two-font system: editorial serif for hero/section statement roles, functional sans for labels, body, navigation, product information, and buttons.
- Avoid adding extra decorative layout types. Reuse the same product rail, editorial grid, story card, and CTA patterns.

## Homepage Design Decisions

- Header becomes a two-row brand header with centered logo and a right-side reservation CTA.
- Hero becomes left-heavy image composition with right-side pale space and vertical copy.
- Hero H1 remains present for SEO but visually hidden; the visible hero message is lower than the page-level H1.
- Existing product carousels remain horizontal with arrow controls and mouse drag.
- Product hover should scale the image inside a fixed frame rather than moving or expanding the card.
